## Observations
- **Frame time (needs a decision).** Menu, instructions, leaderboard, pause and game over run at 10 to 25 fps against 23 to 53 fps in play, in the same browser. Numbers are in the table above. This is a headless software-rendered browser, so a real GPU browser will be faster, but the gap to play is the signal. Two things stand out and I did not change code or tuning to separate them:
  - The three full-window pages (menu, instructions, leaderboard) have no blur (`instructions.blur` and `leaderboard.blur` are false) and still run at 10 to 25 fps. The instructions page is the slowest (about 10 fps) and has the most glass panels. They go through `WindowBackdrop.draw` (`game/ui/window_view.py:34`): it draws the background on a 656 px field, then `pygame.transform.scale`s it to the 856 x 518 window every frame. Play draws the field at its own size and has no such step. That is a candidate for the cost, not a measured cause.
  - Pause and game over (blur on, `glass.blur_down` 4) run at 14 to 16 fps, and game over after the save at 10.7. Blur or the dimmed world plus HUD could both be a part. A test with `pause.blur` and `game_over.blur` set to false in `game/tuning/screens.json` would tell. I am not allowed to edit tuning.
  - Suggested owner: the Architect and the UI Developer decide. The contract (`screens.md`, Rules, blur) says the defaults are "to be measured in the browser build": these are those measurements.
- The picture behind the game-over box is frozen, as the contract says (same drifters in every run's screenshot).
- Playwright's `browser_navigate` away from a running game shows a `beforeunload` dialog and I could not answer it (no dialog tool). I closed the tab and opened a new one each time. Not a game bug, a pygbag page behavior.
- Extra checks not shown: none left from the screens list. The leaderboard "place 4 to 10 with up to ten entries" and the three medal rows were shown with a seeded save. A very long score (more than seven digits) was shown (`1 234 567`).
- The `tick cap hit` DEBUG lines in debug mode follow the slow frames above (dropped 0.02 to 0.09 s).
